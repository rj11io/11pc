# Brand direction

11blog should feel restrained, precise, and reading-first. Its identity comes from strong typography, disciplined spacing, sharp geometry, and a small number of deliberate interface signals.

Use the [color palette](./color-palette.md) as the source of truth for brand colors. Dark mode is the default expression; light mode should preserve the same hierarchy and character.

## Brand context

| Element | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Name | `11blog` |
| Author | `rj11io` |
| Content focus | AI, product engineering, and technology in general |

Current publication names, article copy, author profiles, tags, and image subjects in the repository are placeholders. They must not inform branding decisions unless they are replaced with approved real content or visual references.

## Logo

The primary [11blog logo](./logo/README.md) pairs a geometric double-one mark with a custom, mono-inspired wordmark. Its green signal square is the only accent, keeping the identity aligned with the sharp, restrained interface.

## Visual character

- Use sharp lines, square corners, and crisp edges. The core interface has no border radius.
- Define structure with thin hairline borders rather than shadows or decorative containers.
- Keep surfaces flat and quiet. Use contrast, spacing, and rules to establish hierarchy.
- Use the green primary color selectively for actions, active states, eyebrows, and small signals, not as a large decorative fill.
- Prefer generous negative space and a measured editorial rhythm over dense layouts.

## Typography

- Use a neutral sans serif for body copy, headings, navigation, and interface labels.
- Use a monospaced face for the 11blog wordmark, code, version information, and other technical details.
- Set major headlines large, compact, and confidently weighted, with tight letter spacing.
- Keep body copy comfortable and open, with generous line height and readable line lengths.
- Use small uppercase labels with wide tracking for eyebrows, metadata categories, and section markers.
- Prefer sentence case for page titles, article titles, buttons, and navigation.

## Layout and composition

- Build layouts on a clear grid with consistent alignment between headings, copy, imagery, and controls.
- Use wide page margins and progressively larger spacing between major editorial sections.
- Keep long-form article copy in a narrow reading column, supported by secondary navigation such as a table of contents.
- Let cards and rows share the same visual language: square frames, thin borders, simple metadata, and restrained hover feedback.
- Maintain strong responsive behavior by stacking content naturally rather than compressing desktop compositions.

## Imagery

- Use full-bleed crops inside strict rectangular frames; avoid rounded masks and ornamental treatments.
- When photography is unavailable, use abstract tonal cover art with a restrained gradient, fine texture, and a publication monogram.
- Keep captions, credits, and image controls visually secondary to the image itself.
- Treat image subject matter, photographic style, and art direction as undecided until real content or dedicated visual references define them.

## Components and interaction

- Buttons should be rectangular, direct, and compact. Primary actions may use a solid green fill; secondary actions use a border.
- Selected controls may invert foreground and background colors or use a restrained green-tinted surface.
- Use badges as small status signals with concise labels such as “Featured” or “New.”
- Hover states should rely on subtle border, background, opacity, or position changes.
- Keep motion short and purposeful. Respect reduced-motion preferences and avoid playful or attention-seeking animation.
- Always provide visible keyboard focus, sufficient contrast, semantic structure, and useful alternative text.

## Direction in one line

Sharp editorial structure, disciplined spacing, and a restrained green signal color, all in service of clear reading.
